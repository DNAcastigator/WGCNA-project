# WGCNA-project

This repository documents the RNA-seq and ATAC-seq analyses used to compare gene co-expression and chromatin co-accessibility between *Heliconius erato* and *Heliconius melpomene* during forewing development. The workflow begins with gene- and peak-level count matrices, constructs four species- and assay-specific WGCNA networks, evaluates module quality and cross-species preservation, and integrates the RNA-seq and ATAC-seq results to examine conservation of the *WntA* regulatory network.

The analysis is divided into five sequential parts. Read alignment, alignment filtering, duplicate removal, gene-level counting, peak calling, peak annotation, and conversion to pan-genome coordinates are described in the associated paper and are not repeated here.

## Part 1 — Preparation of RNA-seq matrices for WGCNA

This section prepares the *H. erato* and *H. melpomene* RNA-seq matrices used for co-expression analysis. It begins with the gene-level read-count matrices generated by the RNA-seq pipeline described by Van Belleghem et al. (2023). The analysis retains forewing samples from fifth-instar larvae, day 1 pupae, and day 2 pupae; applies count filtering, variance-stabilizing transformation, and correction for unwanted variation; and performs sample and gene quality control. Reciprocal orthology assignments are then used to place genes from both species in a common *H. erato* gene-name space.

## Part 2 — Preparation of ATAC-seq matrices for WGCNA

This section prepares the peak-level chromatin-accessibility matrices used for co-accessibility analysis. Peaks are associated with their closest RNA-expressed gene and represented in the common pan-genome coordinate system. The analysis retains peaks linked to the forewing-development gene set identified by RNA-seq WGCNA, applies accessibility and variance filters, performs variance-stabilizing transformation and correction for unwanted variation, and conducts sample and peak quality control.

## Part 3 — WGCNA module construction and developmental-stage associations

This section constructs four independent weighted networks: *H. erato* RNA-seq, *H. melpomene* RNA-seq, *H. erato* ATAC-seq, and *H. melpomene* ATAC-seq. Dataset-specific soft-thresholding powers and module-detection parameters are applied using signed-hybrid networks and biweight midcorrelation. Module eigengenes are then related to developmental stages to identify the temporal association of each RNA and ATAC module.

## Part 4 — Module preservation and within-reference module quality

This section evaluates both the quality of modules within the species in which they were identified and their preservation in the other species. RNA-seq and ATAC-seq networks are compared in both reference directions using 100 permutations. The resulting quality, preservation, module-size, median-rank, and composite Z statistics provide complementary measures of module robustness and cross-species conservation.

## Part 5 — *WntA* network comparison and CRE conservation

This section extracts the top 1% of genes most strongly connected to *WntA* in each RNA-seq network and evaluates the significance of the cross-species network overlap. Spearman correlations between *WntA* and shared genes are used to classify conserved and non-conserved expression relationships. These classifications are integrated with ATAC-seq peak homology and developmental-stage associations to test whether conservation of *WntA*-centered expression relationships is accompanied by structural and temporal conservation of associated cis-regulatory elements.

## Analysis order

The files should be followed from Part 1 through Part 5. Parts 1 and 2 generate the processed matrices used in Part 3; Part 3 generates the network objects used in Parts 4 and 5.
